Comparing body mass by species

First, I calculate the average body mass for each species.

average_mass <- penguins_data |>
  group_by(species) |>
  summarise(
    mean_body_mass_g = mean(body_mass_g),
    median_body_mass_g = median(body_mass_g),
    n = n()
  )

average_mass
# A tibble: 3 × 4
  species   mean_body_mass_g median_body_mass_g     n
  <fct>                <dbl>              <dbl> <int>
1 Adelie               3706.               3700   146
2 Chinstrap            3733.               3700    68
3 Gentoo               5092.               5050   119

The results show that body mass differs between the three species. Gentoo penguins have the highest average body mass, while Adelie penguins have the lowest average body mass.

Body mass and flipper length

Next, I examine the relationship between body mass and flipper length.

ggplot(
  penguins_data,
  aes(x = flipper_length_mm, y = body_mass_g, colour = species)
) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.7) +
  labs(
    x = "Flipper length (mm)",
    y = "Body mass (g)",
    colour = "Species"
  ) +
  theme_minimal()

The figure shows a positive relationship between flipper length and body mass: penguins with longer flippers generally tend to have greater body mass. The species also form somewhat distinct groups.

Comparing males and females

Finally, I compare the average body mass of male and female penguins.

sex_mass <- penguins_data |>
  group_by(species, sex) |>
  summarise(
    mean_body_mass_g = mean(body_mass_g),
    .groups = "drop"
  )

sex_mass
# A tibble: 6 × 3
  species   sex    mean_body_mass_g
  <fct>     <fct>             <dbl>
1 Adelie    female            3369.
2 Adelie    male              4043.
3 Chinstrap female            3527.
4 Chinstrap male              3939.
5 Gentoo    female            4680.
6 Gentoo    male              5485.

Across the species, the average body mass differs between males and females. This provides another example of how biological characteristics can vary within and between penguin species.

Conclusion

Using the Palmer Penguins dataset, I found clear differences in body mass between species. Body mass is also positively related to flipper length, and average body mass differs between males and females. These simple summaries and visualizations demonstrate how R can be used to explore relationships in a real dataset.
